# Manager Coaching Cadence Exemplar

## Use Case

Design a sustainable coaching rhythm for a manager whose loudest anecdotal concern conflicts with the available call evidence.

## Fixtures

- [team-context.md](team-context.md): team shape, capacity, existing rhythm, and evidence access
- [current-coaching-pains.md](current-coaching-pains.md): observed behavior patterns, manager hypothesis, and evidence limitations

All names and data are fictional.

## Natural Prompt

> Using team-context.md and current-coaching-pains.md, design a lightweight weekly coaching cadence for the next four weeks. Keep it within the manager's available capacity, focus on the fewest behaviors likely to improve outcomes, and include an observation loop, commitment tracker, and monthly trend review. Explain how you handled the conflict between the manager's hypothesis and the call evidence.

## Expected Output Excerpt

A good response should make the trade-off visible:

| Weekly element | Time | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Evidence triage | 30 min | Sample new calls and select one discovery-impact and one next-step example |
| Two small-group labs | 2 x 35 min | Practice one target behavior with four AEs per lab |
| Commitment review | 20 min | Verify the prior commitment against a new call or follow-up |
| Manager notes and escalation | 20 min | Record evidence, confidence, and non-skill blockers |

The first cycle should prioritize discovery impact and next-step ownership. Pricing objection handling can remain a monitored hypothesis until broader evidence supports it.

## Review Checklist

- Total cadence fits within 4.5 hours per week, including preparation and notes
- No more than two active team behaviors at once
- Each coaching claim points to observed evidence rather than rep reputation
- Each commitment has an owner, due date, and observable verification signal
- Generated weekdays, deadlines, or escalation thresholds are clearly proposed rather than presented as existing policy
- The manager hypothesis is retained as a hypothesis, not dismissed or treated as fact
- Individual performance conclusions are avoided because the sample is team-level and incomplete
- The monthly review separates skill gaps, process gaps, and pipeline constraints
- Evidence-review volume and tooling assumptions make the capacity calculation credible

## Deliberate Uncertainty

Only 12 calls were sampled, two AEs are unrepresented, and no win-rate relationship has been established. The cadence should include continued sampling rather than presenting the initial pattern as a final team diagnosis.
