# Current Coaching Pains

All observations are fictional.

## Call Sample

- Window: previous three weeks
- Calls reviewed: 12 discovery calls across six of eight AEs
- Two AEs had no calls in the sample
- Seven calls did not connect the stated workflow problem to measurable or strategic impact
- Six calls ended with a general follow-up but no customer owner or decision date
- Three calls handled an objection before asking a diagnostic question
- Two calls included pricing pushback; both came from the same rep

## Manager Hypothesis

The manager believes pricing objection handling is the team's largest coaching gap because two recent escalations were highly visible.

## Evidence Tension

The broader sample points more strongly to discovery-impact and next-step-control gaps. Pricing may still matter, but the available evidence is concentrated in one rep and is not yet a team-level pattern.

## Process Signals

- CRM next-step fields are optional and often stale.
- Reps do not share a common definition of a strong next step.
- The manager has no consistent place to record coaching commitments.

## Desired Behavior Changes

1. Ask at least one question that connects workflow pain to business impact.
2. End discovery with a next step that has a customer owner, purpose, and date.
3. Diagnose an objection before responding.

## Evidence Limitations

- This is a small convenience sample, not a representative performance study.
- Call outcomes have not yet been linked to the observed behaviors.
- No conclusion should be made about the two AEs absent from the sample.
