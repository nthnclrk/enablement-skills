# Response to a cheaper competitor

## Prompt and inputs

I've got a call in 30 minutes. Our buyer said: Competitor X is 30% cheaper, so I don't see why we'd pay more. We're selling scheduling software to an operations team. That's all I know about their quote. Our verified capability is routing work between teams with an audit history. I have no approved savings numbers and can't offer a discount. Help me answer them naturally. I don't need a whole playbook.

The prompt is the complete source packet.

### C: Initial skill draft

They may not see enough extra value to justify the price. It could also be a firm budget limit; you don’t know yet.

Start with:

“That’s a fair question. Is the extra cost outside your budget, or have you just not seen enough difference to justify it?”

Pause and let them answer. If it’s about value:

“What I can confirm is that we can route work between teams and keep an audit history. I don’t yet know what’s included in their quote, so I can’t tell you whether that’s a difference. Is that routing and history important to your team? If it is, could we walk through one real handoff and check how each option handles it?”

If it’s a firm budget ceiling:

“Understood. I can’t offer a discount. If our price is above a fixed limit, we should be clear about that before asking you to spend more time evaluating us.”

Your limit: the 30% figure is buyer-reported, not a verified like-for-like comparison. Don’t claim savings or suggest the competitor lacks your capability. The next step is to compare one relevant workflow and what each quote includes, not promise that yours will justify the premium.

### H: Revised skill

Start with:

“That’s a fair question. What’s included in their quote for the work your team needs to do?”

Pause and let them answer.

If cross-team handoffs and traceability matter to them:

“We can route work between teams and keep an audit history. Let’s compare how both options handle that and what’s included in each quote. Then we can see whether there’s a reason to pay more.”

If they don’t know what’s included, suggest checking the quotes against their requirements together before arguing for the premium.

Your limit: don’t claim the competitor lacks those capabilities, promise savings, or offer a discount. The 30% difference is their reported figure, not a verified like-for-like comparison.
