#### Run each 30-minute individual session

1. **Minutes 0–4: review together.** Replay or read the relevant segment. Ask: “What were you trying to achieve here? What context am I missing?” Done when you both understand the example and the rep has had a chance to correct it.
2. **Minutes 4–8: give focused feedback.** Identify one effective behaviour to keep and one change supported by the example. “You clarified who does the work. We still don't know what the delay affects.” Done when the rep can describe the change in their own words.
3. **Minutes 8–20: rehearse and retry.** Play the buyer using the actual scenario. Let the rep try the changed behaviour, give specific feedback, then repeat with a slightly different buyer response. Done when the retry demonstrates the behaviour, or you can name the precise part requiring further practice.
4. **Minutes 20–26: plan application.** Choose the next eligible live interaction. Ask: “Where will you use this? What might get in the way?” Done when there is a realistic opportunity and the rep knows what evidence to retain.
5. **Minutes 26–30: agree the check.** Record the rep's commitment and your follow-up date together. Done when both can say what you'll inspect and when; mark unavailable dates as unresolved, not agreed.

If the rep cannot yet perform the behaviour in rehearsal, simplify the task and practise again. Don't treat an agreed action item as proof of learning.

#### Run the shared practice

- **0–5 minutes:** read the example and define one observable behaviour.
- **5–9:** demonstrate a weak response and an improved response; ask what changed.
- **9–23:** work in pairs. Both reps take a buyer and AE turn, give behaviour-specific feedback and retry.
- **23–27:** hear two retries and check them against the behaviour.
- **27–30:** each rep names where they can use it next. The four reps in individual cycles record this in their existing coaching row; the others retain a brief personal reminder, not a second report.

For week 4, compress the demonstration and pair rounds into 20 minutes and reserve 10 minutes for the learning review. This replaces time within the session; it is not an extra meeting.

Manager: review by the mutually agreed check date.” Fill in real names and dates together. Store links and brief observations, not duplicate transcripts or unrelated personal information. Write the initial row in the session's final four minutes; use the weekly admin block for scheduling and unresolved dates.

#### Follow-up decisions

Use the next eligible sample, not CRM completeness alone:

- **Behaviour used:** give specific feedback and check whether it fits the buyer's response. Continue or close the loop based on the evidence.
- **Opportunity occurred, behaviour absent:** ask what happened; check understanding, practice and workflow barriers before changing support.
- **No eligible opportunity:** reschedule the evidence check. This is missing evidence, not a failed commitment.
- **Call or email is sound, CRM is stale:** handle the recording issue as a process question. Agree the minimum existing field or note to use; request any system change through its owner. Don't assume authority to make fields mandatory.

At week 4, use the already recorded observations to report coverage, comparable examples of application and unresolved checks. Keep group practice participation separate from demonstrated use on calls. If samples are too few or differ materially, report examples and limits instead of a percentage improvement. The pack supplies neither that owner nor an escalation threshold: confirm both before any formal action. No missed-session count or four-week result in this plan is a promotion or performance rule.

#### One simple loop

**Observe → practise → commit → try it with a buyer → check the next example.**

Use the same loop every week:

1. **Before practice:** each rep shares the next eligible discovery call from the agreed weekly window, with two timestamps: the discussion of impact and the close. Ask for no more than six minutes of material in total, a one-sentence context note and an existing follow-up email if it helps establish the next step. They do not need to write a call summary. Choose the next eligible call, not the rep's best call.
2. **Review within your time cap:** mark the three behaviours below and write one useful observation. If context is insufficient, mark it unclear rather than guessing or spending the whole hour on one call.
3. **Practise one move:** use the week's common focus in the groups. Use individual sessions to work on the rep's specific obstacle.
4. **Agree one live-call commitment:** name the action, when the rep will try it and the evidence you will check. Avoid giving each rep three improvement projects.
5. **Check the next eligible example:** next week's evidence review closes the loop on this week's commitment. In week four, schedule that check into the continuing rhythm.

#### What you are looking for

Use these definitions in both practice and call review. Record **met**, **attempted but incomplete**, **not demonstrated despite an opportunity**, or **unclear/no opportunity**. The last category stays out of any success-rate denominator.

| Behaviour | A useful observable test | What is not enough |
|---|---|---|
| Connect workflow pain to impact | The rep asks a relevant impact question and explores the buyer's answer. Record whether the buyer identifies measurable or strategic impact; do not invent it. | Asking a stock ROI question, accepting “it is frustrating” and moving straight to a pitch. |
| Agree the next step | The buyer and rep agree a purpose, a named customer owner and a date. Where relevant, make clear what decision the step will support. | “I'll send something over” or a seller-created calendar invite with no buyer agreement. |
| Diagnose before responding | When an objection occurs, the rep asks a relevant question, listens to the answer and uses it to shape the response. | Giving a rebuttal before understanding the concern. No objection means no observation, not a failure. |

Separate the rep's action from the buyer's response. A good impact question may not produce a usable answer immediately. Coach the follow-up, while recording that the impact is still unconfirmed.

Examples to model:

- Impact: “When that handover breaks down, what happens to the team's delivery target?” Then follow the answer: “How often is that happening?” or “Which priority does that put at risk?”
- Next step: “To check whether this solves the handover issue, could we review the current workflow with Priya next Tuesday? Would you bring her into that discussion?” Confirm the owner, purpose and actual date together.
- Diagnosis: “When you say the price is too high, what are you comparing it with?” Use this only when it fits the buyer's concern; it is not a universal script.

#### The four weeks

**Commitment:** “On my next discovery call, when a buyer describes a workflow problem, I'll ask how it affects their work or a business priority and follow the answer once. I'll share the timestamp.”

**Done by Friday:** all eight reps have a practice turn and one commitment; each has an initial observation or an explicit evidence gap. You have not labelled anyone's readiness from this exercise.

#### How to run each meeting

##### The 35-minute group practice

- **5 minutes:** name the behaviour and explain why it matters, using one anonymised example.
- **5 minutes:** show a weak attempt and a stronger attempt. Ask the group what changed.
- **20 minutes:** four five-minute rounds. Each rep gets two minutes as the seller, one minute of specific feedback and two minutes to retry. Rotate the buyer role; everyone else watches for the same behaviour.
- **5 minutes:** each rep states one action they will use and the live opportunity where they will try it.

Feedback format: “At this point you did X. The buyer responded Y. On the retry, try Z.” Discuss observable choices, not traits such as confidence or attitude. Keep sensitive individual feedback out of the group.

##### The 20-minute individual session

- **3 minutes:** “What were you trying to achieve? Where did you get stuck?”
- **5 minutes:** inspect the relevant excerpt together. Ask the rep to assess it first.
- **7 minutes:** rehearse the difficult moment, give one adjustment and retry.
- **5 minutes:** agree one live action, the evidence and the review point. Update the tracker together.

If the conversation turns to deal status, note it for the pipeline meeting and return to the skill. If the issue is access, workload or a broken process, name that blocker; more role-play will not fix it.

#### Keep the admin to one small tracker

Use an existing private team document. Eight rows are enough:

| Rep | Current commitment and intended call/date | Evidence link + timestamp | W1 / W2 / W3 / W4 observation | Next check / blocker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Confirm purpose, customer owner and date on the next discovery call | Link and close timestamp | W3: attempted; owner still unclear | Check next eligible call; rehearse owner confirmation |

Each weekly observation can be a short code for the three behaviours plus one factual sentence. Keep prior weekly observations so the month-end comparison does not rely on memory. Reps add their link and commitment; you add the observation. No separate coaching deck, full-call summary or duplicate CRM report is needed.

Keep recording links and individual notes in the approved internal location with appropriate access. Share anonymised excerpts in group practice, and do not copy customer material into a broader-access tracker or external tool.

#### Your decision rules after four weeks

- **Behaviour is appearing in repeated live examples:** keep a light check and choose the next narrow improvement. A single good role-play is not proof of transfer.
- **The same gap persists after two coached attempts:** use the next individual session to test the cause. Is the rep missing the skill, struggling to recognise the moment or facing a process constraint?
- **Evidence is missing or unclear:** keep the conclusion open and collect the next eligible example. Do not turn absence of evidence into a performance finding.
- **Pricing concerns recur across several reps:** investigate the pattern and whether it concerns diagnosis, value, buyer fit or commercial terms before changing the whole team's focus.

The test of this pilot is simple: can each rep name the one behaviour they are working on, show where they tried it and explain what they will change next—and can you run that loop inside 270 minutes? Keep the rhythm if the answer is yes. Adjust the practice focus from the evidence, not from whichever escalation was loudest.

#### Run every individual session in 25 minutes

Use **one behaviour**, not a call scorecard.

**Impact drill:** buyer says, “We spend too long reconciling reports.” The rep asks, “What does that delay affect?” then follows the answer towards measurable or strategic impact.
