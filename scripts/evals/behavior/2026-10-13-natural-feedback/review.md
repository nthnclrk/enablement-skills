# Independent artifact review

## Assessment basis

The four dimensions are:

- **Evidence fidelity:** accuracy against the excerpt, including restraint about what remains unknown.
- **Domain judgment:** whether the feedback serves the stated call goal and interprets buyer cues appropriately.
- **Practical usability:** whether a manager can deliver the feedback and the rep can practise it and observe progress.
- **Proportionality:** whether the scope, language and detail fit the requested short coaching conversation.

Each uses the requested artifact scale: **0** harmful or unusable; **1** needs major repair; **2** needs minor repair; **3** ready as delivered. These scores are not an overall release grade. Word counts use whitespace-separated words.

## A versus B

**Preference: B, narrowly.** It more directly connects both premature moves—proposing invoice sync at T4 and defaulting to a demo at T6—to the call's unfinished goal of agreeing what to evaluate. Its final buyer question gives the rep a clear way to turn the explored impact and IT approval process into that agreement. The distinction between an initial metric and an unknown business impact is also easy to convey in a 1:1.

A is also ready to use and has the more explicit next-call listening check, plus a useful question about approval ownership. The preference is about how directly B supports this requested coaching conversation, not its length. The shared scores reflect that neither answer requires repair.

## Limits

This is one comparison of A and B for one discovery excerpt, plus a separate assessment of C for one demo excerpt. It establishes only the usefulness and fidelity of these delivered artifacts against the supplied requests and assertions. It does not establish consistency across other cases, coaching effectiveness in practice, rep readiness or overall release quality.
