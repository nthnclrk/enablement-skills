# Stage exit judgment

Use this when you are writing stage cards or governance. The skill body has the procedure. One live deal belongs in `deal-inspection-coach`.

## Buyer signal, not activity

A stage is a buyer state. Exit criteria prove that state. Required proof is the minimum evidence that blocks movement.

**Fails.** Stage 3: Proposal. Exit when the proposal is sent and a follow-up meeting is on the calendar.

**Passes.** Stage 3: Commercial evaluation. Exit when the buyer confirms the proposal addresses their named decision criteria and a dated review with the economic buyer exists. Sending the deck is activity.

A scheduled meeting, sent proposal, completed demo, or populated field is activity until buyer evidence shows what changed. Missing proof is not negative evidence.

## What each card must name

- Buyer state the stage represents
- Deal risk this stage reduces
- Entry evidence from the prior stage
- Exit evidence that is buyer-validated
- Required proof that blocks progression
- Recommended proof that coaches only
- Who may approve an exception, and what they must record
- Anti-patterns: seller assertions that do not prove progression

Keep required proof minimal. Do not require a field, artifact, or meeting unless it proves the buyer state or a necessary operating decision.

## Qualification anchors

Use qualification elements as evidence checks where they reduce this stage's risk:

- economic buyer
- measurable pain
- decision process and timing
- competition or status quo
- champion strength
- implementation feasibility

Do not reprint the full method on every card. Stakeholder access or business-case evidence is a motion-specific hypothesis, not a universal late-stage gate. Validate blocking proof against segment, channel, and history before making it required.

## Backtest before you enforce

Compare current and proposed rules on won, lost, no-decision, and open deals:

| Check | What to report |
| --- | --- |
| Progression | Would progress, remain, regress, or need an exception |
| Separation | Whether criteria distinguish outcomes or risk states |
| Operability | Evidence availability, manager interpretation, seller burden |
| Forecast effect | Aging, conversion interpretation, category consistency |

If history is incomplete, the model is a hypothesis. Define a bounded pilot. Do not present an untested criterion as predictive.

## Governance and rollback

Exception: reason, approver, evidence, expiry or review trigger, reporting treatment.

Inspection cadence follows deal volume, stage duration, and the team's existing rhythm.

Record baseline and success thresholds for completeness, exception rate, aging, conversion, and forecast variance. Name the decision owner, review point, rollback trigger, and path back to the prior model.

Do not make CRM fields required, change automation, or move live opportunities without explicit approval and a tested migration plan.

## Thin evidence

If stage history is missing, you can still write buyer-signal cards. You cannot claim they will improve conversion. Leave historical findings unknown.

## Optional shape

```text
Stage / buyer state / risk reduced
Entry / exit
Required proof (blocks) / recommended proof (coaches)
Exception owner
Anti-patterns
```
