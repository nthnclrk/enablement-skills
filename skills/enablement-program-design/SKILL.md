# Enablement Program Design

Turn a defined commercial problem into a program that changes observable field behavior.

## Confirm Inputs First

Confirm the decisions that shape the program:

- Business problem, evidence, affected outcome, and urgency
- Roles, segments, regions, and managers in scope
- Current behavior, target behavior, and where the change must show up in the workflow
- Sponsor, accountable program owner, approvers, and cross-functional contributors
- Timeline, delivery capacity, budget, systems, and fixed dependencies
- Available baselines, evidence sources, and review cadence
- Confidentiality, customer-data, and customer-facing approval boundaries

Use user-supplied current context or `.enablement/context.md` when available and relevant. Treat stale, inferred, or conflicted context as provisional, and never update shared context silently.

Ask only for missing inputs that change scope, feasibility, or approval. Assumptions may support a reversible planning draft. Keep source facts, metrics, quotes, commitments, dates, approvals, requirements, and claims unknown until verified. If the problem, accountable owner, audience, or decision authority is unknown, produce a discovery brief rather than a final program charter.

## Read The Right Reference

Read [references/program-charter-template.md](references/program-charter-template.md) before drafting the program.

## Default Workflow

1. **Diagnose the performance problem.** Separate evidence from interpretation, distinguish a skill or knowledge gap from process, product, incentive, capacity, territory, or management causes, and state what remains unknown. If enablement is not a material cause or response, stop with the evidence and route the issue to the appropriate owner instead of manufacturing a program.
2. **Define the target change.** Specify the critical behavior by role and workflow moment, the evidence that it occurred, the intended business outcome, and explicit exclusions.
3. **Design the minimum viable program.** Match each intervention to a diagnosed barrier. Combine only the assets, practice, manager reinforcement, workflow support, and communication needed to produce the target behavior.
4. **Set the capacity envelope.** Estimate audience volume, facilitator and manager time, content-production effort, system constraints, and support load. Reduce scope or sequence cohorts when the plan exceeds available capacity.
5. **Build the pilot.** Define cohort selection, entry conditions, duration, evidence collection, participant safeguards, and the decision rule to continue, adjust, or stop.
6. **Plan phased delivery.** For design, pilot, rollout, and reinforcement, name the owner, dependencies, entry and exit criteria, review point, and confirmed date or unresolved scheduling decision.
7. **Establish governance.** Assign decision rights, approval gates, escalation paths, and change-control ownership. Keep internal guidance separate from customer-facing material and route regulated or sensitive claims through the authorized reviewer.
8. **Define measurement.** Connect program activity to the target behavior and one primary business outcome. Record metric definitions, baseline status, data source, owner, limitations, and decision thresholds; do not invent baselines or targets.
9. **Stress-test execution.** Check adoption friction, manager burden, regional or role differences, data quality, conflicting initiatives, and what will happen if dependencies slip.
10. **Produce the charter and operating plan.** Make unresolved decisions, evidence gaps, and next approval points visible.

## Tool Notes

- Keep the design tool-agnostic. Map approved work into existing project, learning, CRM, content, and reporting systems when useful.
- Do not create records, publish materials, enroll participants, or change system configuration without explicit approval of the destination and content.
- Minimize personal and customer data. Use aggregated or de-identified evidence when individual-level detail is unnecessary.

## Output Contract

Default output includes:

- Decision brief: problem, evidence status, target behavior, outcome, scope, and open decisions
- Program charter with exclusions, sponsor, owner, contributors, and decision rights
- Intervention map linking each barrier to the minimum required response
- Capacity, dependency, and owner plan
- Pilot design with entry, exit, and continue-adjust-stop criteria
- Phased rollout and reinforcement plan
- Measurement plan with definitions, sources, baselines, limitations, and review cadence
- Risk register and approval log

Label the artifact as a working draft until hard-stop decisions and approvals are resolved.

## Quality Bar

- Address a demonstrated performance problem, not a training request by default.
- Use this as a program wrapper only when coordinated workstreams need shared scope, governance, capacity, pilot, and measurement; do not absorb a well-defined specialist job.
- Make the target behavior observable in the seller or manager workflow.
- Keep scope inside the available delivery and manager capacity.
- Tie every intervention to a diagnosed barrier; remove activities that do not earn their place.
- Give each phase an owner, decision gate, and evidence-based exit criterion.
- Make reinforcement part of the operating rhythm rather than a post-launch reminder.
- Keep facts, interpretations, assumptions, and unresolved decisions distinct.
- Make the program usable by a first-time owner while preserving enough evidence and governance for senior review.
