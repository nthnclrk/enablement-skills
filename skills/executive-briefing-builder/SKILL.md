# Executive Briefing Builder

Prepare leaders to make or secure a specific decision without losing control of facts, authority, or sensitive material.

## Confirm Inputs First

Confirm:

- Meeting type, date, duration, objective, and the decision or commitment sought
- Attendees, roles, decision authority, relationships, and confirmed priorities
- Account or business history, current state, and source packet with freshness
- Preferred outcome, minimum acceptable outcome, non-negotiables, and walk-away or escalation conditions
- Authorized asks, offers, concessions, claims, and approval owners
- Speaker roles, format, distribution audience, and confidentiality boundary

Use user-supplied current context or `.enablement/context.md` when available and relevant. Treat stale, inferred, or conflicted context as provisional, and never update shared context silently. Account-specific evidence takes precedence over general context for account facts.

Ask only for missing information that changes the decision frame, authority, or external message. Assumptions may support reversible internal planning. Keep source facts, metrics, quotes, commitments, dates, approvals, requirements, stakeholder positions, and customer or product claims unknown until verified. If the target decision, key attendee authority, commercial approval, or shareability boundary is unresolved, label the brief as internal working material and block the affected external section.

## Default Workflow

1. **Set the decision frame.** State the decision owner, decision sought, desired outcome, minimum acceptable outcome, unresolved approvals, and what must happen next if no decision is reached.
2. **Build the evidence base.** Reconcile dates, numbers, commitments, and account events against named sources. Separate confirmed fact, interpretation, planning assumption, conflict, and unknown.
3. **Map the room.** Record each attendee's role, authority, priority, likely concern, and desired movement. Leave unverified positions unknown instead of treating them as fact.
4. **Shape the narrative.** Reduce the case to the current situation, material impact, recommendation, evidence, and why the decision matters now. Remove points that do not support the meeting objective.
5. **Control asks and offers.** Pair every ask, offer, concession, and fallback with authority, conditions, approval status, and escalation trigger.
6. **Plan the conversation.** Assign the opener, evidence sections, questions, objection handling, decision check, close, and note owner. Allocate time around the decision rather than presentation volume.
7. **Separate distribution layers.** Keep negotiation guardrails, stakeholder hypotheses, internal risks, and fallback limits in the internal brief. Create a distinct customer-shareable extract only when requested and approved.
8. **Run the pre-flight check.** Verify names, roles, figures, dates, links, claims, speaker ownership, open approvals, and confidentiality labels. Surface contradictions rather than resolving them without evidence.

## Tool Notes

- Use approved CRM, contract, BI, call, and document sources when available; record provenance and freshness for decision-critical evidence.
- Minimize personal and customer data. Include only information needed for the meeting and appropriate for the named distribution group.
- Do not send the brief, share customer-facing material, update an account record, or make a commitment without explicit approval of the content and destination.

## Output Contract

Default output includes:

- One-page executive core: objective, decision requested, recommendation, material evidence, principal risk, and open approvals
- Evidence and source-status notes for decision-critical facts
- Audience and decision-authority map
- Strategic narrative and supporting proof
- Timed meeting plan with speaker and decision checkpoints
- Ask, offer, concession, fallback, and escalation matrix
- Likely concerns with evidence-based response guidance
- Internal-only, customer-shareable, and restricted-content labels
- Open questions, conflicts, planning assumptions, and briefing owner

Put detailed evidence in an appendix when it would make the core brief harder to use.

## Quality Bar

- Make the requested decision clear enough to repeat in one sentence.
- Let a leader grasp the brief's core in a few minutes.
- Reconcile every material number, date, commitment, and claim to a named source or mark it unknown.
- Distinguish attendee facts from hypotheses about their position.
- Keep asks and concessions inside confirmed authority.
- Give each meeting segment an owner and purpose.
- Keep internal strategy impossible to mistake for customer-facing content.
- Preserve dissent, uncertainty, and fallback conditions instead of forcing false alignment.
