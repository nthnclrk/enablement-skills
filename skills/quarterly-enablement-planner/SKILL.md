# Quarterly Enablement Planner

Turn quarterly priorities into a realistic enablement plan with owners, milestones, and review points.

## Confirm Inputs First

Confirm:

- Quarter and planning horizon
- Business priorities and major initiatives
- Teams and roles covered
- Known launch dates and immovable events
- Gross capacity by owner or constrained role, committed BAU, carryover, leave, and any protected contingency
- Candidate-initiative evidence, dependencies, and required design, SME, delivery, reinforcement, and measurement effort
- Supplied current context or relevant sections of `.enablement/context.md`, when available; stale, inferred, or conflicted material remains provisional

If inputs are partial, ask for missing blockers. Use labeled assumptions only for reversible sequencing; never invent capacity, fixed dates, ownership, or commitments to make the roadmap fit, and leave work unscheduled when owner capacity is unknown.

## Read The Right Reference

Read [references/quarterly-plan-template.md](references/quarterly-plan-template.md) before building the plan.

## Default Workflow

1. Set the planning scope and evidence boundary from supplied current context and source material.
2. Translate business priorities into enablement outcomes and target behaviors.
3. Build the net-capacity ledger by owner and time period before prioritizing work.
4. Score business impact and strategic necessity with anchors; rate evidence confidence and effort separately.
5. Classify each item as `must do`, `should do`, `validate`, or `defer` using the reference rules.
6. Fit initiative load to the most constrained owners, including reinforcement and measurement—not only asset production.
7. Sequence work around real decision dates, dependencies, peak selling periods, and field absorption capacity.
8. Assign owners, milestones, decision gates, and due dates.
9. Define review points at meaningful decision dates and explicit re-plan triggers.
10. Produce a capacity-backed roadmap plus a short execution brief.

## What To Avoid

- Quarter plans that exceed real capacity.
- Using a priority label to pretend mandatory work has capacity.
- Prioritizing visible activity over high-impact work.
- Omitting explicit defer calls, which creates hidden scope creep.
- Failing to define re-plan triggers when launches or priorities shift.

## Tool Notes

- Keep planning format tool-agnostic.
- If requested, map deliverables into the user's existing calendar, project tracker, or wiki.
- Do not write dates or assignments into external systems without explicit user approval.

## Output Contract

Default output includes:

- Quarterly enablement roadmap or calendar
- Anchored initiative decision table with value, confidence, effort, and rationale
- Capacity ledger by constrained owner and time period
- Defer list with rationale and re-entry conditions
- Owner and milestone matrix
- Decision-date review cadence and re-plan triggers
- Risk and dependency notes

## Quality Bar

- Calendar is achievable with available capacity.
- Every scheduled initiative has named owner capacity for design, review, delivery, reinforcement, and measurement.
- High-impact work is prioritized ahead of low-value activity.
- Reinforcement and inspection are built into the quarter.
- Defer decisions are explicit enough to prevent hidden overcommitment.
- Low-confidence high-potential work is validated before receiving full delivery capacity.
- Plan is clear enough for cross-functional alignment.
