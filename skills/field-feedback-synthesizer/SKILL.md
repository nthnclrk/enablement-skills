# Field Feedback Synthesizer

Turn a defined body of field feedback into decision-ready findings without overstating what the sample can prove.

## Confirm Inputs First

Confirm:

- Decision or research question the synthesis must inform
- Included sources, timeframe, teams, segments, and explicit exclusions
- Counting unit and denominator: records, respondents, accounts, opportunities, or calls
- Source identifiers, dates, available metadata, and known collection bias
- Existing taxonomy, output audience, decision owner, and required cuts
- Consent, confidentiality, retention, and quotation boundaries

Use user-supplied current context or `.enablement/context.md` when available and relevant. Treat stale, inferred, or conflicted context as provisional, and never update shared context silently.

Ask only for missing inputs that would change the sample, coding, confidentiality, or decision. Assumptions may support a reversible analysis plan. Keep source facts, metrics, quotes, commitments, dates, approvals, requirements, identities, and claims unknown until verified. If the corpus, counting unit, or decision question is unresolved, produce an analysis plan rather than quantified findings.

## Read The Right Reference

Read [references/feedback-coding-framework.md](references/feedback-coding-framework.md) before coding the corpus.

## Default Workflow

1. **Define the sample.** Record the research question, inclusion and exclusion rules, collection method, counting unit, denominator, coverage, and known blind spots.
2. **Create a source register.** Assign stable record and source IDs, preserve source date and relevant segment metadata, and remove unnecessary personal or customer information.
3. **Normalize without erasing provenance.** Preserve the original statement or faithful excerpt alongside normalized text. Leave missing metadata unknown.
4. **Remove copied duplication.** Collapse exports, reposts, and repeated copies of the same originating statement. Keep independent observations separate and report both mention count and independent-source count when useful.
5. **Calibrate the codebook.** Start from the decision question, test the taxonomy on a small mixed subset, resolve overlapping codes, and record changes before coding the full corpus. For consequential analysis, have a second reviewer check a sample.
6. **Code the evidence.** Apply theme, segment, workflow stage, evidence type, and severity tags consistently. Keep frequency, severity, evidence strength, and business impact as separate dimensions.
7. **Quantify with the right denominator.** Show `n/N`, base sizes, coverage, and independent-source breadth. Do not imply population prevalence from a convenience sample or compare segments with incompatible coverage.
8. **Look for variation and contradiction.** Preserve minority, role-specific, regional, and disconfirming signals that an overall ranking could hide. Report cuts only when the base supports them.
9. **Interpret cautiously.** Separate observation, interpretation, and root-cause hypothesis. Name the next evidence needed before treating a hypothesized cause as established.
10. **Recommend the next decision.** Prioritize actions by impact, evidence confidence, urgency, reversibility, effort, and owner capacity. Include a validation step and decision point for each material recommendation.

## Tool Notes

- Use spreadsheets or analysis tools for repeatable coding and counts when the corpus warrants it; preserve a source-to-code trail that a reviewer can inspect.
- Automated classification or sentiment may assist triage but does not replace review of material findings, contradictions, or sensitive excerpts.
- Do not write findings into product, CRM, performance, or planning systems or distribute verbatim feedback without explicit approval of the content, audience, and destination.

## Output Contract

Default output includes:

- Scope and coverage note with counting unit, denominator, source mix, exclusions, and limitations
- Decision summary that distinguishes findings from hypotheses
- Theme table with `n/N`, independent-source breadth, segment cuts, severity, confidence, and evidence references
- Representative, minimally necessary excerpts with confidentiality treatment
- Minority, contradictory, and missing signals
- Recommended actions with owner, rationale, validation step, effort or capacity note, and decision point
- Codebook and compact source register sufficient for review
- Open questions, unresolved conflicts, and planning assumptions

## Quality Bar

- Trace every material finding to included records without exposing unnecessary sensitive detail.
- Count originating signals rather than duplicate copies.
- Report denominators and coverage wherever frequency could influence a decision.
- Keep frequency, severity, impact, and confidence distinct.
- Preserve important segment and minority signals even when they are not the largest theme.
- Treat root cause as a hypothesis until evidence establishes it.
- Do not generalize beyond the sampled population or imply precision the source mix cannot support.
- Make each recommendation proportionate to the evidence and executable by a named owner.
