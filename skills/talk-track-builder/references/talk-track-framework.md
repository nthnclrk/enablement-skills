# Talk track judgment

Use this when you are writing or checking lines. The skill body has the procedure.

## Spoken-line test

Read each line out loud. Keep it if a seller would say it to a real buyer without looking at the page. Cut it if it sounds like a landing page, a slide title, or a coaching note.

A line fails the test when it:

- Thanks the buyer for their time, then pitches
- Uses "align," "leverage," "unlock," "visibility," or "outcomes" as the point
- Describes a topic ("discuss ROI") instead of giving words
- Needs a second breath, or a semicolon, to finish

**Fails.** "I'd love to align on how we can help you optimize cross-functional handoff visibility."

**Passes.** "Where does a handoff go to die today, and who finds out?"

**Fails.** "We help operations leaders drive accountability across complex workflows."

**Passes.** "When a handoff is missed, who gets the escalation, and how late are they finding out?"

## One moment

Do not mix discovery and demo into one track. Diagnose until you know what "good" looks like for this buyer. Demo only against that. If both are needed and neither architecture exists, stop and use `discovery-and-demo-structure`.

Discovery asks what is breaking and what it costs. Demo shows the one path that matches that answer. Commercial talks value against price and procurement. Renewal talks outcomes they already have, and the risk of losing them. Manager inspect is for coaching a track, not for selling.

## Branch pattern

For each likely turn: trigger you hear, diagnostic question, then the response. Proof only if it is approved and on-point. Then recover the ask.

**Fails.** Buyer: "We already have a process." Seller: "I understand. Other customers felt that way and then saw 28% fewer missed handoffs."

**Passes.** "That makes sense. Is the issue that the process is missing, or that it becomes hard to inspect once work crosses teams?"

Use proof after the diagnosis, and keep the limitation in the sentence:

"In a three-customer pilot, unresolved handoffs fell 28% within 60 days. That is directional, not a benchmark for your environment."

Usual turns, only when this moment will hit them:

- "We already have a process"
- Skepticism that this is more tooling
- Price or budget
- Competitor already in the account
- "The team will not adopt another system"

## Proof

Keep each spoken proof under 20 seconds. Name the population, the timeframe, and the limit. If the evidence is a pilot, say pilot. If it is one customer, say one customer.

No source: write `needs source` and ask a different question instead.

Do not turn a standard-integration result into a custom-ERP promise. Do not turn a directional metric into a guarantee. Do not invent logos, pricing, or time-to-value.

## Thin evidence

If you lack impact numbers, economic buyer, or timing, the track should discover those, not paper over them. Leave the unknown in the question path:

"I do not have a number for what a missed handoff costs you. What happens downstream when one lands late?"

## Optional shape

Use this only when the user wants a scanned card. The lines still have to pass the spoken-line test.

```text
Moment / role / persona / time
Objective (spoken)
Through-line (spoken beats)
Proof (line, source, scope, or `needs source`)
Branches (trigger → diagnose → respond)
Landmines
Next-step ask (spoken)
```
