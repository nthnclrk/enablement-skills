# Discovery And Demo Framework

Use this framework to design conversations that produce decision clarity.

## Meeting Modes

- `Discovery only`: prioritize decision context, problem depth, impact, stakeholders, criteria, and next validation.
- `Demo only`: confirm prior discovery, show selected proof, test fit, and resolve open evaluation questions.
- `Combined`: reserve enough time to validate context before choosing what to demonstrate. Remove demo blocks that discovery does not support.

## Discovery Topic Pattern

For each priority topic, define:

1. `Reason`: the decision uncertainty this topic should resolve.
2. `Open`: one natural question that invites the buyer's view.
3. `Listen for`: evidence, ambiguity, conflict, and language worth reflecting back.
4. `Deepen`: a follow-up about impact, cause, priority, or decision consequence.
5. `Branch`: explicit if/then guidance for what to ask, show, or skip based on the answer.
6. `Capture`: the fact, requirement, hypothesis, or unresolved question to record.

Do not ask every prompt. Move forward when the decision uncertainty is resolved; probe further when the answer is material but vague.

## Discovery Decision Areas

- `Change`: what changed, why this is being considered now, and what happens if nothing changes.
- `Current State`: workflow, friction, affected groups, and existing alternatives.
- `Impact`: operational or business consequence and how the buyer evaluates it.
- `Desired State`: outcome, constraints, and acceptable trade-offs.
- `Stakeholders`: users, evaluators, approvers, blockers, and missing perspectives.
- `Criteria`: functional, technical, commercial, security, and adoption requirements.
- `Process`: evaluation steps, ownership, approvals, and timing rationale.

Treat unknown authority, timing, requirements, and impact measures as questions, not completed fields.

## Demo Selection Rule

Include a demo block only when it has:

- a verified buyer outcome, concern, or decision criterion,
- a verified capability or workflow to show,
- an approved proof point or a clear statement that proof is still needed, and
- a question that tests whether the demonstration changed the buyer's confidence.

Sequence blocks by the buyer's decision logic, not the product menu. Defer a block when discovery is insufficient, the environment is unverified, or the capability claim is not approved.

## Demo Block Pattern

- `Reflect`: restate the relevant buyer statement and check that it is still accurate.
- `Frame`: explain the outcome or risk the block addresses.
- `Show`: demonstrate the shortest workflow that proves the point.
- `Test`: ask what fits, what does not, and what evidence remains necessary.
- `Branch`: define what to do if the buyer confirms fit, raises a mismatch, needs specialist validation, or disengages.

## Close Decision

1. Recap buyer-confirmed outcomes, criteria, and unresolved questions.
2. Ask whether the meeting resolved the intended decision.
3. If yes, propose the next decision step and confirm owner and timing.
4. If partly, agree on the evidence, stakeholder, or validation still needed.
5. If no, capture the mismatch and avoid manufacturing momentum.

Label timing as `buyer-confirmed`, `internally committed`, or `proposed`. A calendar event is not proof of a buyer commitment unless the buyer agreed to it.
