# Objection Library Builder

Build a reusable objection playbook that helps teams respond consistently without sounding scripted.

## Confirm Inputs First

- Use supplied current context or relevant sections of `.enablement/context.md` when available. Treat stale, inferred, or conflicted material as provisional; never update shared context silently.
- Confirm segment, persona, and sales stage scope.
- Confirm current objection sources (calls, notes, CRM fields, manager input).
- Confirm the qualified objection register or its coverage: usable sources, observed count, unique-account breadth, segment or stage mix, and concentration limits.
- Confirm prevalence, business or deal impact, influenceability, and evidence confidence separately.
- Confirm proof sources available (customer outcomes, product evidence, policy terms).
- Confirm legal, pricing, or procurement constraints that affect responses.
- Confirm the library owner, approval roles, and events that should trigger review.
- If inputs are incomplete, ask for only the minimum missing items. Use labeled assumptions only for reversible taxonomy or workflow choices; never invent verbatim wording, frequency, proof, source facts, or approval status.

## Read The Right Reference

- Read [references/objection-library-framework.md](references/objection-library-framework.md) when you need response structure, severity definitions, and escalation rules.

## Default Workflow

1. Preserve the evidence.
Capture representative objection wording verbatim or clearly mark it as paraphrased. Record source reference, date, persona, stage, and transcript confidence before normalizing it; anonymize customer and employee identifiers when they are not needed.
2. Normalize without overwriting.
Create a short objection label and analyst interpretation while retaining the source wording separately.
3. Group by theme.
Cluster objections by persona, stage, competitor, and buying concern.
4. Prioritize the list.
Record prevalence, business or deal impact, influenceability, and confidence separately, then apply the priority matrix in the reference. Keep a rare blocking concern visible even when prevalence is low. If raw calls still need coding or corpus analysis, route that work to `call-insights-to-objections` first.
5. Write the response path.
Use a simple flow: acknowledge, diagnose, answer, prove, and confirm the next step.
6. Add diagnostic questions.
Include one or two questions that help the rep uncover whether the objection is real or hiding a bigger issue.
7. Add proof and collateral.
Link each response to the best proof point, case study, product fact, or policy. Record source, approved wording, owner, last-verified date, and approval state.
8. Add do-not-say guardrails.
Call out the phrases that create overpromising or confusion.
9. Add lifecycle governance.
Assign each play card an owner, version, status, last-reviewed date, and event-based review triggers. Retire superseded responses instead of leaving multiple active versions.
10. Add escalation rules.
Make it clear when the rep should hand off to legal, security, pricing, or implementation.
11. Route adjacent work:
   For strategic messaging, use value-prop-and-positioning.
   For competitor-specific counters, use battlecard-builder.
   For practice drills, use roleplay-scenario-builder.
   For raw multi-call coding and prevalence analysis, use call-insights-to-objections.

## Output Contract

- Objection taxonomy with priority tiers.
- Source-linked objection evidence register with verbatim or labeled paraphrased examples.
- Response play cards for each tier-one objection, preserving evidence separately from interpretation.
- Proof and collateral map with provenance and approved wording per response.
- Lifecycle status, owner, version, last-reviewed date, and review triggers per play card.
- Escalation matrix by objection type.
- Coaching notes for managers.
- Assumptions list when source inputs were incomplete.

## What To Avoid

- Treating all objections as equal without prioritization by deal risk.
- Shipping rebuttals without diagnostic lines that uncover root cause.
- Publishing responses without ownership or approval status.
- Converting cleaned-up wording into a fake quotation or presenting sparse evidence as frequency data.
- Leaving outdated, superseded, or unapproved responses active alongside the current version.

## Quality Bar

- Responses are evidence-backed and commercially realistic.
- Library distinguishes diagnosis from response.
- Escalation boundaries are explicit and enforceable.
- Priority reflects business risk, not opinion volume.
- Reps can apply responses quickly in live calls.
- Every quotation and proof claim is traceable to a source, and transcript uncertainty remains visible.
- Product, pricing, policy, legal, or proof changes trigger review before continued use.
